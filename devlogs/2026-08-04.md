## Lean pass (corrected to true FPS lean)

Left/right lean is an authoritative eye state, not a cosmetic tilt: the head offset moves the shot origin sideways so peeking cover actually clears it, keeping the crosshair truthful.
True FPS lean: the first-person gun visibly tilts by rotating around its front sight tip (so the sight picture and crosshair stay true), the character silhouette tilts, and the camera horizon always stays parallel to the ground. There is no camera roll.
Lean travels in the input frame as a clamped integer and the network protocol is bumped to 7; older frames omitting the field are rejected.
The HUD offers two movable lean controls with hold (follow the finger) and tap (latch until tapped again) modes, selectable in settings, plus Q/E keyboard lean for desktop. Lean works from every stance including prone.
Settings: `LEAN ADS` (on by default) makes leaning aim on its own; `ADS LOOK` and `LEAN LOOK` (off by default) let the finger holding the ADS or a lean button also drag-steer the camera. The earlier camera-roll "view shift" dials were removed as wrong.
Remote silhouettes show the lean as a torso roll and head shift, plus a subtle strafe-lean derived from networked lateral velocity so locomotion reads as animation.
Snapshots carry lean as a discrete (never blended) field so replicas tilt correctly.
The render target is raised to 120 Hz (`run/max_fps=120`, iOS `enable_high_refresh_rate`, `physics_ticks_per_second=120`); actual frame rate still depends on device capability.
The new `riftline_lean_exercise.gd` locks the frame field, authoritative eye offset, lean-from-prone, snapshot round-trip, hold/tap HUD semantics, auto-ADS, and network rejection of lean-less or stale-protocol frames.
Lean preview captures `lean-left` and `lean-ads` were rendered at 2622x1206 and inspected: the gun tilts with the front sight on the crosshair and a level horizon.
All 18 exercises pass and the host/client 5v5 LAN smoke runs clean at protocol 7 and 120 Hz, with only the pre-existing ENet MTU warning for the large unreliable snapshot packet remaining as a known optimization target.
